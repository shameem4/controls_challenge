Controller Test Drive

Watch a controller steer a real segment. The road is drawn from the commanded lateral acceleration; the car drifts when it fails to produce it. Run others as ghosts to see where they part company.

Six controllers in two families. Three close the loop on lateral acceleration (pid, ff_pi_boot, cnn_v4); three close it on trajectory — reconstructed position and heading error — so lateral acceleration is emergent (pid_traj, ff_pi_traj). Each trajectory controller shares a parent with a lataccel one and differs only in the error signal, so the pairs are directly comparable. Watch the wheels, not the road: trajectory control is smoother at the wheel and worse at the line, and the road view cannot show the first half of that because drawing a path double-integrates the error away.

How to read the drift. The benchmark scores lateral acceleration error, not position, so integrating that error freely over 40 s would show metres of wander the cost never penalises. The lane offset here is a leaky integral (≈3 s memory) of the heading error (a_achieved − a_commanded) / v — corner drift reads true, while long-run drift does not accumulate fictitiously. Costs shown are the real benchmark numbers from tinyphysics, steps 100–500.
